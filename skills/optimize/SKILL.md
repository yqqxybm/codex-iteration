# Optimize

Optimization is the practical judgment and implementation of a change that lets
an existing object more fully become what it is for.

Understanding comes first: the object, its purpose, its concrete situation and
relations, the tension governing its present failure or unrealized possibility,
and the change it can responsibly bear. Reality both inspires and corrects this judgment.
Process carries it through complex work. Neither is the subject of optimization.

## Entry Judgment

Do not route by the word "optimize" alone. Before starting, determine what the
user is actually trying to make possible and which understanding is missing.
An existing target gives inquiry a concrete starting point, not a form that
must be accepted or preserved. Understanding its limitations and discovering
what it could become belong to the same judgment. Its mechanism, relationships,
or overall form may need reconstruction within the user's purpose and boundary.

Choose an inquiry owner for the question still unresolved:

- software product or project direction: `project-lifecycle`, which may choose
  discovery or brief work when user reality or product commitment is unresolved,
  and project analysis for solution judgments within an accepted commitment;
- consequential non-project direction or decision: `three-step-analysis`;
- fuzzy, lightweight non-project framing: `co-star`;
- textual expression whose direction is already accepted: `self-refine`.

Ask the user only when their purpose or the object cannot be responsibly
inferred and that uncertainty changes the practical choice. Otherwise state the
assumption and proceed, open to correction.

## Ownership And Routing

Use this skill for authorized optimization of an existing non-project target.
It may frame the judgment and coordinate handoff, but does not seize another
domain's actions or state.

- software-project targets enter `project-lifecycle` and use
  `references/software-project-optimization.md`;
- review-only requests use `review` and do not edit;
- retrospective learning uses `retrospective` when the object is future
  practice rather than the current artifact;
- novel work remains with the novel skill family.

Read the owning skill before acting. Preserve its authorization, edit,
verification, state, commit, deployment, and handoff rules.

Read `references/deep-optimization.md` only when the user asks for deep,
best-possible, structural, or review-informed optimization. Ordinary work does
not require the deep reference.

## The Optimization Judgment

Form a compact, revisable judgment before editing. It should make clear:

- what the object is and whom or what it serves;
- its purpose, situation, relations, and constraints;
- what must be preserved because it already realizes that purpose;
- the dominant contradiction, limitation, or unrealized possibility;
- the real alternatives, including the possibility of leaving it unchanged;
- the chosen practical change and why it better realizes the purpose;
- what result or consequence would show the judgment mistaken.

Do not reduce this to a score, checklist, or a default preference for a smaller
change. Let the governing relation determine the fitting scale: sometimes a
deletion, sometimes a redesign, and sometimes a refusal to change. Add rules,
artifacts, abstractions, documentation, or compatibility only when they serve
the governing purpose.

For prompts, skills, agents, and configurations, connect wording to expected
behavior under realistic use. Do not turn one anecdote into a universal ban;
identify the mechanism, the conditions in which it matters, and how a future
outcome could revise the rule.

## Practice

1. **Understand the whole.** Read the target and the context that gives it
   meaning. Locate the relevant users, history, dependencies, constraints, and
   pressures rather than classifying surface defects.
2. **Find what governs.** Identify the contradiction or possibility that most
   determines whether the object realizes its purpose. Explore genuinely live
   alternatives when the choice is material.
3. **Commit to a change.** Make the authorized change through its owner. Keep
   it coherent with the object; let the governing cause and the changed whole
   determine the scale and need for further iteration.
4. **Return to reality.** Check the consequence most capable of challenging the
   judgment. The check is proportional to risk, novelty, and blast radius. A
   text reread may be enough; a behavior claim may need a scenario, test,
   source, or runtime check.
5. **Judge again.** Read the changed whole, not merely the diff. Continue only
   while an in-scope change has a credible causal case for better realization of
   the purpose. Stop when the work is adequate for its situation, the remaining
   uncertainty is named, or the boundary prevents more.

## Review-Informed Work

Review can diagnose a target and supply material for optimization, but it is not
a compulsory pipeline. Keep review read-only and distinguish defects from
possible improvements. Act only on findings whose relation to the governing
judgment is clear and authorized.

Re-review or close out when a change could alter a key judgment, introduce a
meaningful consequence, or leave the original concern unresolved. Do not add a
second review merely because a workflow diagram says so.

## Completion

Report the practical judgment, what changed, why it serves the purpose, what
was preserved, the decisive verification, and material uncertainty or residual
risk. Include supporting detail only when it helps the user decide, sustains
necessary continuity, or was requested.
